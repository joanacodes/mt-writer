# Maison Tarot — brand book, v2

Version 2 keeps everything you approved in v1 and expands it: more examples, more situations, the full vocabulary, the rules for titles, metadata, images, social, email, podcast, and a checklist. Two rules changed at your request and run through the whole document: **the place is called "Maison Tarot" — in English too, never "the House" — and you speak as "I", never as "the host".**

---

## 1. What Maison Tarot is

**One line.** A place where you learn to read the cards, and then learn you never needed them.

**Thesis.** There are no Chosen Ones. Cards aren't magic, you are.

**Taglines.** Two, with different jobs.

- *Demystify mysticism.* — the institutional line. Under the wordmark, in the footer, on the YouTube banner, on the podcast cover, in the meta title of the home page. French: *Démystifier le mystique.* Both languages keep their own; never mix on one surface.
- *Cards aren't magic, you are.* — the personal line. Ends a talk, opens a workshop, signs a post, closes the About page. French: *Les cartes ne sont pas magiques, vous l'êtes.* It's the sentence people will quote back to you, so it never appears twice on one page.

Rule of use: the institutional line where Maison Tarot speaks as a place; the personal line where you speak to a person. Never both on the same screen.

**Why teaching, not reading.** You started by sharing with the people around you. They were curious, so you made them try and stayed beside them while they read — and the moment that matters is when they're shocked by what they've just said. That state can't be described, only experienced. So Maison Tarot makes people try before it explains. Teaching thrills you more than reading; reading stays, free, at events.

**Purpose.** Fewer people scammed. Fewer people afraid of the cards. More people who read for themselves and don't pay anyone any more.

**2036.** The people who came through Maison Tarot read without cards. Maison Tarot is where they hang out. Tarot was kindergarten.

**The place.** A home first. Then a school, a library, an apothecary, a tea house, a Victorian manor that smells of wood, tea and plants. A fun place where you get to read. This is the mental image behind every choice of word: warm, ordered, a little old, no incense.

## 2. Two voices: Maison Tarot, and you

The v1 device was "the House and the Host". It stays, renamed, because the two registers are still useful.

**Maison Tarot speaks** when the site does structural things: welcoming, explaining what's here, stating rules, describing offers, the footer, the disclaimers, the community rules. It says its own name or "here". It never says "we" as if there were a staff.

> Welcome to Maison Tarot. Here you learn to read the cards, test whether it works, and keep whatever holds up.

> Maison Tarot doesn't read on health, death, pregnancy, court cases or money. Not because a card can't fall on them, but because a wrong word there does damage.

**You speak** — "I", "je" — for everything that comes from experience: the method, the numbers, the articles, the readings, opinions, the origin story, feedback to students. Where a name helps ("I, [name], read for…") the name is yours to give; the writer tool has a setting for it. Never "the host", never "the author", never "she".

> I've read for 300 strangers who told me nothing. I kept count. About one in ten misses, and I publish those too.

**Never** "nous"/"we" implying staff. If Maison Tarot ever has a second person, this section is rewritten.

**The relationship between them** allows the occasional aside — "Maison Tarot doesn't do curses. Neither do I, but I'm asked less often." — without turning the site into a bit. One aside per page, at most.

**How to tell which voice a sentence needs.** If deleting the sentence would make the page *less clear*, it's Maison Tarot's. If deleting it would make the page *less true*, it's yours.

## 3. Personality

At her best: **sharp, honest, understanding.** To avoid: **blunt.** Attentive and honest stay. The difference between sharp and blunt is whether the reader feels seen or hit; the test is that a sharp sentence could be said to a friend across a table.

What we take from Ryanair and Oatly: **self-aware honesty.** Know what you are, say it before anyone else does, and be funny about it. What we don't take from Macy's: meanness. Competitors are colleagues until their behaviour is dangerous; then we describe the mechanism, not the person.

Fixed traits, with what they look like on the page:

- **Elegant, modern, no trendy slang.** An educated adult talking to another one. *Yes:* "It calls you out more often than it flatters you." *No:* "It'll drag you, respectfully."
- **Illustrated.** Every rule comes with an example, every concept with a situation. A section without an example is unfinished.
- **Humble by design.** "Don't take my words as ultimate truth. Test it and experience it for yourself." No messiah. Every claim carries its own way of being checked.
- **Curious rather than right.** Questions are the point. "I don't understand this part yet" is a sentence that appears on the site.
- **Anti-hierarchy, anti-dependency.** No ranks among readers or students, no "advanced" people, no certification, no reason to come back that isn't wanting to.
- **Honest about misses, without theatre.** A miss is stated, counted, and left. No apology spiral, no "the cards work in mysterious ways".

## 4. Voice rules

### French
- **Vous**, always: clients, students, blog, social. Cordial, never stiff. The vous of a good host, not of a bank.
- Examples and illustrated situations in every explanation.
- Precise and warm. Humour is dry and lives in asides, not punchlines.
- No swearing. None.
- The French is written, not translated: its own idioms, references and rhythm. When the English uses an English example, the French finds a French one.

### English
- Same person, more of her. You articulate yourself better in English, so the English carries more personality, more dry humour, a touch more directness.
- Contractions yes. Slang no. British spelling.
- Still no swearing.

### Both
- **No ALL CAPS.** Ever. The wordmark is the only capitalised thing on any surface.
- **Exclamation marks:** at most one per page, only where a human would actually raise their voice a little.
- **Emoji:** none in site copy. On social, one at most, only when it does a job.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "300 readings, about 90% by my count" beats "hundreds of accurate readings". But the 300 appears only where the count is the subject — not as a refrain.
- **Say what you don't know.**
- **Disclaimers are promises, not fine print.** Same voice as everything else, and the humour is allowed there.
- **Short paragraphs.** One idea each. A paragraph longer than five lines on a phone is two paragraphs.
- **Verbs over nouns.** "You'll learn to read a card you've never seen" beats "acquisition of a reading methodology".
- **The reader is capable.** Never "simply", "just", "easy". If it were easy they wouldn't be reading.

## 5. Tone by situation

| Situation | The rule | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Welcoming a first visitor | Reassure without selling | "Here you learn to read the cards, test whether it works, and keep what holds up." What's refused is on the first screen too. |
| Teaching | Practice first, theory as lenses | "Look at the card. What colour is everywhere? Say it. Now here's why that matters." |
| A reading misses | Own it, learn from it, don't rescue it | "That didn't land — tell me where I went wrong." Refund if paid. Never "it'll make sense later". |
| Someone is in distress | Stop, refund, redirect | "A reading isn't the right tool for right now." Name the kind of professional, not a diagnosis. |
| A sceptic | Don't argue, reframe | "Knowing the future isn't the point. Reading yourself is. And here's my count, misses included." |
| A true believer wants certainty | Refuse gently, give the lens instead | "Set-in-stone answers don't exist. Here's what I can give you." |
| A believer wants a tribe | Refuse clearly | "No cult, no us-against-the-world, no ranks. Bring questions." |
| A journalist | Polite, factual, numbers, no drama | Offer the method page. Nothing off the record. |
| Talking about competitors | Colleagues, until dangerous | Name behaviours ("finds a curse, sells its removal"), never people, unless people are the danger. |
| Someone wants you to decide for them | Refuse, kindly | "I read; you decide. Keep training that muscle." |
| Someone bases their life on readings | Refuse, redirect | "Take a break from the cards. Not everything is spirits; sometimes it's a therapist." |
| Someone asks about their ex, their boss, a third party | Refuse, explain | "I read you. The other person isn't in the room and hasn't asked." |
| Someone offers money for a reading | Refuse, redirect to lessons and free events | "I don't sell readings. I teach, and the readings are free at events." |
| Praise ("you have a gift") | Deflect to the method | "Everyone has it. I just stopped trying to be right. Come and try." |
| A mistake on the site | Fix, note, no drama | "Corrected on [date]: the previous version said X." |

## 8. Money

Prices feel like something you can gift yourself or someone else: worth it, and it's going to be fun. Not "premium", not "an investment", not "cheap". No discounts to create urgency, no countdowns, no "only 3 spots left" unless literally true, and even then said plainly.

Free, always: the Library, the Readers' Guide, online reading events. Paid: sessions and workshops. Never readings.

**Affiliate links** (decks, books): allowed on deck pages and articles, with a one-line disclosure in the same voice at the top of the page: "Some links here earn Maison Tarot a small commission. It doesn't change the price, and it doesn't change what I say about the deck." Only decks you've held. Star ratings are yours, signed, never presented as a community average.

**Ads:** possible on Library and blog pages only, never on offer pages, not before traffic justifies it.

## 9. Formatting rules for articles

- **The summary** (the short-answer box) at the top: 60–110 words, the direct answer, self-contained. The hurried reader stops there, satisfied.
- **H2s** as statements or questions a reader would type, with an anchor. Direct answer in the first hundred words of each.
- **Bold** for the one sentence to remember in a section. One per section, at most.
- *Italics* for nuance, for card names on first mention, for your sayings when not in a quote block.
- Quote blocks for signature lines and for anything you'd put your name under.
- Lists only for steps, red flags, and "what to do". Otherwise prose.
- Tables for data and correspondences.
- No underline. No highlight in body copy (the tool doesn't render it).
- A closing section with one concrete next step and a link — the learn path, the method, an event, or a related article. Never a summary of what was just said.
- An honest limit stated where relevant: what this doesn't prove, what I don't claim.
- Length by intent: 800–1,000 words for a question with one answer; 1,400–1,700 for a subject that earns it (a scam guide, the Death card, mental health).
- Links: four to seven per article, descriptive anchor text, never the same target twice, never in headings.

## 10. Titles, metadata, and how the search engines see you

**Titles.** Keyword first, promise second, under 65 characters where the keyword allows. Patterns that work here: the direct question with an answer implied ("Is tarot a sin? Tarot and religion, from someone raised between two"), the "without" ("How to read tarot cards for beginners, without memorising 78 meanings"), the number ("9 red flags"), the honest bracket ("(for a while, at least)"). Never clickbait, never a question you don't answer.

**Meta descriptions.** 140–165 characters, a real sentence, containing the keyword, ending with a reason to click that is true.

**Summaries** are written for the AI answer engines as much as for people: a complete answer in one paragraph, no links, no "in this article".

**Categories** are in the article's language: Learn / Apprendre, Practice / Tirages, Ideas we refuse / Idées reçues, Trends / Tendances, Astrology, tested / Astrologie testée, For readers / Pour les tarologues, Card meanings / Signification des lames, Stories & experience / Récits, Method / Méthode, Seasonal / Saisonnier.

**Tags**: five to seven, in the article's language, keyword-shaped ("reversed tarot cards", not "reversals").

**Slugs**: keyword-led, short, ASCII, fixed before writing so every link resolves. Each pair of articles shares one `translationKey`.

## 11. Images

The full rules are in the visual charter. For copy purposes: photographs are black and white until touched, cards are always in colour and whole, no mystical props ever. Each article has one cover prompt built from a specific subject plus a fixed style suffix and negative list, so four hundred covers read as one site. Alt text describes the photo in plain words, in the article's language, and is never the prompt.

## 12. Social

**Instagram / Threads.** One idea per post. The caption is written like the site, not like Instagram: full sentences, no emoji unless one does a job, no hashtag wall (three at most, in a comment). Signature lines are the posts that travel. Card posts show the card whole, in colour, with the number and element as text. Never a "pick a pile" with a private reading in the bio.

**TikTok / Reels.** Same voice, shorter. The honest formats: one card read live with nothing said first; "that's a miss" said on camera; a myth dismantled in thirty seconds. Never "your person is coming back".

**YouTube.** Titles follow the article rules. Thumbnails: photo left, black panel right, title in Schibsted Grotesk. No arrows, no open mouths, no red.

**Facebook.** Events and the reviews page. Reviews are quoted with consent and dated; none are invented.

**Reddit.** As a reader, for months, before ever posting a link. Only where it answers someone.

**Medium / Substack.** Secondary publication, under your name, with the canonical link to the site. Never first.

---

## 13. Email

**Transactional** (booking, guide download): short, in the same voice, no upsell. "Your seat is booked. Here's what to bring: nothing. Tell me nothing when you arrive."

**The Guide's follow-ups**: at most one email a month; each one teaches something or announces an event; never a countdown, never "last chance". No affiliate links in email, ever (Amazon's rule and yours).

**Replies to people**: you, first person, and the refusals stated as kindly in email as at the table.

---

## 14. Podcast

The tagline under the cover: *Readers talking to readers.* / *Des lecteurs qui parlent aux lecteurs.* One guest, one lens, one honest conversation. The intro is yours, in the first person, thirty seconds, no "welcome back to another episode". Misses are said on air. Guests are colleagues; the difficult ones are asked the mechanism, not challenged on their sincerity.

---

## 15. Writing an offer

A session, a workshop or an event is described in this order: what you'll do (a card in the first ten minutes), what you'll leave with (a method, not a list), the practical facts (length, size, price in your currency, slots in your time zone), what's refused, and the promise. Never the benefits list, never "transform your practice", never testimonials that aren't dated and consented.

> Workshop: Colours. Three hours, online, twelve people at most. You learn to read a card you've never seen from its colours alone. You leave with a method, not a list to memorise. Priced in your currency, slots in your time zone. You'll leave knowing more than when you arrived, and needing me less.

## 16. The origin story — what goes where

**On the About page**, the short version: you came to astrology to mock it, friends gifted you a deck, you read for people around you and were blown away, and within a year you'd read for 300 strangers and kept count. Curiosity turned into method.

**Not on the About page**, but in your own articles when you choose: the voice that gave a name, the accident, the friend who died, the dreams. Told with your full honesty about what you don't understand, and never as proof of anything.

**Never public**: "she's alive and we're not friends any more."

---

## 17. What Maison Tarot never does

- No hierarchy among readers or students. No ranks, no "advanced", no certification.
- No dependency: no subscriptions to readings, no "you need a second session", no fear.
- No cult, no us-against-the-world, no messiah.
- No object worship: decks are cardboard; the deck-cleansing industry gets an article, not a shop.
- No readings on health, death, pregnancy, active legal matters, money decisions. No curses. No third parties.
- No mean marketing. No urgency theatre. No capital letters.
- No answers set in stone.
- No invented stories, reviews, or numbers. What's published was counted.

---

## 18. Before publishing — the checklist

1. Does the summary answer the question on its own?
2. Is there one sentence in bold per section, and no more?
3. Does every claim have a way of being checked, or a stated limit?
4. Is the number 300 there only because the count is the subject?
5. Any banned word? Any "the House", "the host", "simply"?
6. Are the links four to seven, descriptive, all resolving?
7. Does it end on a next step, not a recap?
8. Would you say every sentence to a friend across a table?
9. Is the French written, not translated?
10. Would the reader leave needing you less?
