I read cards, I teach people to read them, and I spend a surprising amount of my time helping people who've been fleeced by someone who claimed to. The patterns repeat so exactly that you can learn them in ten minutes. Here they are, roughly in the order you'll meet them.

## 1. They find a curse {#curse}

Or a block, an attachment, a dark energy, a "negative entity", a generational hex. The name changes with the fashion; the structure never does: there is something on you, only they can see it, only they can remove it, and removal costs money. Often the first reading is free or cheap precisely so this can be discovered.

This is not a grey area. It is the oldest con in the trade, it is illegal in most countries as fraud, and there is no version of it that's legitimate. The moment you hear it, leave. [I refuse the subject entirely](/disclaimers/#refusals), and so does every reader I respect.

## 2. They create urgency {#urgency}

"We need to deal with this today." "The window is closing." "I don't usually say this, but…" Fear plus a deadline is the second half of the curse move, and it appears on its own too: pressure to book the next session before you've left the first.

Nothing in a reading is urgent. A card doesn't expire. A reader who needs you to decide now needs your money now.

## 3. They're never wrong {#never-wrong}

Watch what happens when something doesn't land. A real reader says "that's a miss" and moves on. A fake one reframes: "the cards are showing something you haven't realised yet", "this may be about someone close to you", "it will make sense later". Every statement becomes unfalsifiable, which means none of them were ever readings.

I miss about one time in ten and I say so at the table. The number matters less than the willingness to say it.

## 4. They ask for your details first {#details-first}

Your name, your birth date, your question, your partner's name, your job, "just so I can tune in". Every one of these is material for cold reading — the technique of feeding your own information back to you dressed as insight. The more they ask, the less they're reading.

The opposite is the test I use. Tell the reader nothing. If they can't work without your context, they weren't working with anything else.

## 5. There's always a next step {#next-step}

The free reading leads to the paid one. The paid one leads to the "deeper" one. The deeper one needs a "clearing", which needs a follow-up. Legitimate readers may see clients more than once, but the shape is different: you come back because you want to, not because the last session ended on a cliffhanger.

At Maison Tarot there is nothing to sell after a reading, because [readings aren't sold at all](/method/) — only lessons. That's a structural choice, and it's the simplest way I know to remove the incentive.

## 6. They promise outcomes {#outcomes}

"He will come back." "You'll be pregnant by spring." "The money is coming." Predictions with dates and guarantees are the product of platforms, not readers. Nobody can promise an outcome, and the readers who do know exactly what they're doing: they're selling relief now and won't be there when the date passes.

## 7. They flatter {#flatter}

You're special. You're an old soul. You have a gift. Your energy is rare. Flattery is the cheapest way to make someone feel read, and it's a reliable sign that the reader is managing you rather than looking at cards. [A real reading is not always pleasant](/blog/is-tarot-evil/); it has told me things I didn't want to hear, and that's most of its value.

## 8. They read subjects no ethical reader touches {#subjects}

Health diagnoses, death, pregnancy, an ongoing court case, someone else's private life, "is my partner cheating". Not because a card can't fall on these, but because a wrong word does real harm and no reader is right every time. A reader who'll answer anything is a reader who hasn't thought about being wrong.

## 9. The reviews are perfect {#reviews}

Five stars, hundreds of them, all recent, all saying "amazing, so accurate, she knew everything". Real practices have uneven reviews, quiet months, a complaint or two. Perfection is manufactured, and it's cheap to manufacture.

## What a real reader looks like {#real-reader}

Someone who tells you what they won't read and why. Who says "that's a miss" without flinching. Who asks you nothing before starting. Who has a price, states it, and has no add-ons. Who can describe [how they check their own accuracy](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/). Who tells you that a reading is not a reason to act, and means it.

## If you've already paid {#already-paid}

Stop paying. Don't argue with them; they're better at this conversation than you are. Keep the messages. If it's a platform, report it to the platform and to your consumer authority. If it's a "curse removal", that's fraud in most jurisdictions, and it's worth a report even if you don't expect the money back — the next person might be spared.

And don't let it put you off the thing itself. The con works because the honest version works, and the honest version is [learnable](/learn-tarot/) — which is the surest way to never be fooled again.
