I need to tell you about the video before I tell you the answer, because the answer is the video.

## The video {#the-video}

Late 2020. I'd already had my ego handed to me by astrology — I'd put a video on to mock it and it had described me too precisely to laugh at. So when a "pick a card" tarot reading turned up in my recommendations, I was more sceptical, not less. A reading recorded weeks ago, for everyone, and I choose a pile? Come on.

I pressed play anyway, because I'd learned what happens when I'm sure.

Silence. Then I was crying. Not tearing up — sobbing. The reader, Kloee Taylor, was saying my situation. Not vaguely: the exact shape of it, the thing I hadn't been able to explain to anyone, the reason I felt like I was speaking another language from everyone around me. I'd never heard someone else put words on it. And partway through, she started crying too. It felt, absurdly, like we were synced.

I've called it the beginning of my awakening, and I've also written that I find that word cringe — [both are true](/blog/spiritual-awakening-the-word/). What I know is that a recorded reading for thousands of strangers reached one of them completely, and that stranger was me.

## So do they work? {#do-they-work}

Yes, in the way a song works.

A song is recorded once, for everyone. Nobody wrote it about you. And yet sometimes a line lands so exactly on your life that you have to stop the car. That isn't the songwriter reading you; it's a well-made thing meeting a person who was ready for it. Pick-a-card readings are that. A reader lays out a general situation with enough specific texture that some fraction of the audience will recognise themselves in it — and for that fraction, the recognition is real. It was real for me.

What they aren't is a reading *of you*. The reader didn't know you existed. The pile you chose didn't know either. What happened is that you brought your life to a picture and the picture was good enough to hold it.

## What separates the good ones from the rest {#good-from-rest}

Three things, in my experience of watching a lot of them since.

**Specificity over flattery.** The bad ones tell every pile that they're special, that someone is thinking about them, that abundance is coming. That's [Barnum](/blog/why-tarot-readings-feel-accurate/), spread across four piles. The good ones commit to something — "you left a job you were good at and you're pretending it was a relief" — and accept that three piles out of four won't relate.

**No funnel.** The video that changed me sold me nothing. No "book a personal reading for what this means for you", no "extended version on Patreon". [The moment a free reading exists to lead somewhere](/blog/before-a-free-online-reading/), it stops being a gift.

**The reader's willingness to be wrong out loud.** "If this isn't you, that's fine, skip to the next pile." The ones who say it are the ones worth watching.

## What to do with one that lands {#what-to-do}

Write down what landed, in your own words, before the video ends. Then close the app. Don't watch the "extended" version, don't book the private session, don't watch six more piles looking for the same feeling — that's [the road to the fidgety place](/blog/why-you-should-quit-tarot-reading/), and I've seen it from both sides.

What you were given was a moment of being seen. That's rare and it's enough. If it makes you want to understand how it happened, that's the better use of it: [learn to read](/learn-tarot/), and one day be the person who lays out four piles that reach a stranger crying in her bedroom in Paris.

Kloee Taylor, if you ever read this: thank you. You are part of the beginning, and I've been trying to pass it on since.
